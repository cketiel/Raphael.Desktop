Google Maps — usage, cost and savings

The panel that shows what Google Maps is costing, what the cache is saving, and lets you change the two settings that move both figures.

referenceNew in 1.5.0

Every time the system asks Google how long a drive takes, or where an address is, that costs money. This panel shows how much, how much was avoided, and lets you change the settings that decide both.

Administrators only.

Summary#

Five figures, for the period you pick at the top:

CardWhat it means
Bought from GoogleRequests that were billed
Served from cacheRequests our own database answered, at no cost
Cache hit rateWhat share of the total the cache answered. Higher means cheaper
Estimated costWhat the period should cost, with a projection to month end
Avoided costWhat the cached requests would have cost

The daily bar chart is the one that tells the story: the red band is what you pay, the green band is what you do not. Red shrinking while green grows is exactly what a cache is for. Below it, the trend line should climb and then level off — those are the origin-destination pairs that repeat week after week, which is what dialysis and recurring treatment look like.

The cost figures are an estimate, not the invoice. Google's price bands are monthly and project-wide: a period that is not a calendar month prices a volume Google never saw. And if another application uses the same key, it eats the same free allowance.

Detail#

Lifetime totals carry no date filter: every request since counting began.

The per-product breakdown switches between table and chart with the toggle in the corner. Two columns are worth explaining:

  • Free remaining is for the current calendar month, not the selected period. It is the figure you set Cloud Console quotas against.
  • Reported marks the products counted by the application rather than the server: the map load and the autocomplete, which talk to Google straight from the screen.

What is served from cache and what is not. The pin-drag geocoding and the details of a chosen address are asked of the database first, and only bought from Google when nobody has asked before. The map load cannot be cached: it is not a piece of data but Google's library running and pulling images from their servers. Every opening of a screen with a map is a billed load, and the only thing that reduces it is not opening one needlessly. At normal volumes this stays inside the 10,000 free loads a month.

Export CSV writes the whole period — products and days — to your Documents folder.

Settings#

Traffic mode#

ModeWhat it doesPrice
MaxSavingsAsks for routes without traffic and adds our own margin$5 per 1,000 · 10,000 free/month
PrecisionAsks for Google's predicted traffic for the trip's hour$10 per 1,000 · 5,000 free/month

Switching to Precision costs twice as much and halves the free allowance. The cache still works the same, but every request it misses is billed at double.

Cache#

  • Retention days: how long a Google answer is served and kept, from 1 to 3,650 days. Google's terms describe a 30-day window for cached content; the default of 365 is a deliberate business decision, not an oversight.
  • Traffic margin (%): the percentage added to the free-flow time in MaxSavings mode. Raise it if drivers are consistently late at peak hours.

Changes take effect system-wide within a minute, with no release. Nothing needs restarting.

Pricing#

Google's band table lives in the database rather than in code: when Google changes its prices, they are corrected without shipping a version.

What this is actually for#

  • Setting Cloud Console quotas. Read "Free remaining" and the month-end projection, and put the daily limit a little above the real rate. If the quota trips one day, that is not a nuisance: it is the alarm that something went into a loop.
  • Justifying the optimisation work. The bar chart and the avoided-cost figure are the evidence, with dates on it.
  • Spotting a regression. If the cache hit rate drops sharply, something started asking for routes it did not ask for before, or the cache is emptying too early.
