Google Maps — Summary

The period's five figures, the chart comparing what was bought against what the cache served, and the savings trend.

referenceNew in 1.5.0

The money view. Everything here belongs to the period chosen in the bar at the top, and every card and chart carries that period underneath it — so a screenshot of a single chart still says which dates it is talking about.

The period#

The four buttons — This month, Last month, 30 days, 90 days — stay marked in blue, so which one is applied is always visible. Pick dates by hand in the two selectors and the label becomes Custom, with no button marked.

It opens on the current calendar month, which is the period Google actually bills.

The five figures#

CardWhat it means
Bought from GoogleRequests that were billed
Served from cacheRequests our database answered, at no cost
Cache hit rateWhat share of the total the cache answered
Estimated costWhat the period should cost, with a projection to month end
Avoided costWhat the cached requests would have cost

Avoided cost is the figure that justifies the whole optimisation, and it is calculated honestly: those requests are priced from where the real spending left off, because that is the band they would have fallen into. Pricing them from the free tier would have inflated a number somebody is going to check.

Underneath, in small type, is what share of the total spending the cache absorbed.

The two charts#

Requests per day is the one that tells the story. The red band is what you pay; the green one is what you do not. Red shrinking while green grows is exactly what a cache is for. The toggle in the corner switches it to a table.

Cache hit rate trend should climb over time and then level off: those are the origin-destination pairs that repeat week after week, which is what dialysis and recurring treatment look like. A sharp drop here is the sign that something started asking for routes it did not ask for before, or that the cache is emptying too early.

The yellow notice#

It is not decoration. The cost figures are an estimate, not the invoice, for three real reasons:

  • Google's price bands are monthly; a period that is not a calendar month prices a volume Google never saw.
  • The bands are project-wide; another application using the same key eats the same free allowance.
  • Three of the six products are counted from what the application reports, not from what the server measures.
