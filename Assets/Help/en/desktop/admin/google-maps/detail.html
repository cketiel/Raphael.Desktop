Google Maps — Detail

Lifetime totals with no filter, the breakdown by Google product, and what is served from cache and what cannot be.

referenceNew in 1.5.0

The analysis view: where the money goes, product by product.

Lifetime totals#

The first card carries no date filter. It is every request since counting began, with the range of dates it spans. This is the figure that answers "how much have we saved altogether?".

Breakdown by product#

One row per Google product. The toggle in the corner switches between the table and a pie chart. Two columns need explaining:

  • Free remaining is for the current calendar month, not the period chosen above. That is deliberate: it is the figure you set Cloud Console quotas against, and quotas are monthly.
  • Reported marks the products the application counts rather than the server, which are therefore approximate.

What is served from cache and what is not#

This part is worth understanding properly, because the products do not all behave the same:

ProductDoes it go through the cache?
Routes (times and distances)Yes, always
Geocoding (addresses and dragging a pin)Yes, always
Place Details (choosing a suggested address)Yes
Autocomplete (while typing)No
Dynamic Maps (loading the map)It cannot be

The first three are asked of our own database first, and only bought from Google when nobody has asked before. The dialysis clinic picked hundreds of times a month is bought once.

The map load cannot be cached, and that is not an oversight. It is not a piece of data with a value to store: it is Google's library downloading, running in the browser and pulling images from their servers. Storing those images is also forbidden by their terms. Every time a screen with a map is opened, that is a billed load, and the only thing that reduces it is not opening one needlessly. At normal volumes this stays comfortably inside the 10,000 free loads a month.

Exporting#

Export CSV in the top bar writes the whole period — the per-product breakdown and the day by day — to your Documents folder. A consumption report ends up in an email sooner or later, and retyping a table by hand is how figures get wrong.
