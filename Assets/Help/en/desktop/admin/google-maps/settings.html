Google Maps — Settings

The traffic mode, the cache retention and the margin, with what each decision costs.

how-toNew in 1.5.0

These three settings decide what the business pays Google every month. Leaving Precision on by accident doubles the routing bill; dropping the retention to 30 days means every address is bought again a month later. Nobody else in the system can correct this: it is an administrator decision, and it has a price.

Three settings that change what the system costs. Changes take effect across the whole ecosystem within a minute, with no release and nothing to restart: the server re-reads them on its own.

Traffic mode#

ModeWhat it doesPrice
MaxSavingsAsks for routes without traffic and adds a margin of our own$5 per 1,000 · 10,000 free/month
PrecisionAsks for Google's predicted traffic for the trip's hour$10 per 1,000 · 5,000 free/month

⚠️ Precision costs twice as much and gives half the free allowance. The cache still works the same, but every request it misses is billed at $10 instead of $5. The panel shows an orange warning while Precision is on, so nobody leaves it there without knowing.

When to use each:

  • MaxSavings for day-to-day operation. The margin compensates for traffic reasonably well on routes that repeat.
  • Precision when investigating why the times do not add up, or during a season with unusual traffic. Switch back to MaxSavings afterwards.

Cache#

Retention days — how long a Google answer is served and kept, from 1 to 3,650 days. Default 365.

Google's terms describe a 30-day window for cached content. The value of 365 is a deliberate business decision, consistent with the coordinates the customers table has been storing since the system went live. Lowering it to 30 is a matter of changing this number and saving: it takes effect on the next cleanup pass, with nothing to deploy.

Traffic margin (%) — the percentage added to the free-flow time in MaxSavings mode. Default 12. Raise it if drivers are consistently late at peak hours; lower it if they always arrive too early and the routes end up slack.

Google pricing#

The last table is Google's price list, one product per row and one band per column, the way Google publishes it. Routes Pro is in bold because it is the one that costs double and gives half the free allowance.

These prices live in the database, not in the code: when Google changes them — it did in March 2025 and will again — they are corrected without shipping a new version of the program.

Using this to set quotas#

With the mode and the margin decided, go to Detail, read "Free remaining", check the month-end projection on Summary, and set the daily limit in Cloud Console a little above the real rate. If the quota trips one day, that is not a nuisance: it is the alarm that something went into a loop.
