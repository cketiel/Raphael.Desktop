AI access (MCP)

What the AI access (MCP) field is, what a user needs to use their AI assistant with Raphael, how you grant it, what each level shows and how an assistant Raphael does not recognise yet is approved.

how-toNew in 1.18.1

With the AI access (MCP) field you decide whether a user can ask their AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Grok or another) for their company's reports in their own words, and with which level of data. The assistant queries Raphael read-only and with the same scope that user has in the application.

Nobody has this access until Raphael's administrator grants it.

When you need it#

  • A company's director asks to use their AI assistant with Raphael's data.
  • A user changes role, loses their phone or leaves the company, and their access must change or go.
  • A user tells you their assistant "is not recognised by Raphael".

What the MCP is, in one sentence#

MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the standard AI assistants use to connect to outside systems. Raphael has its own MCP server: the assistant asks it what data there is, writes the query, and Raphael decides which rows and columns it gets back. A Provider only ever sees its own data, never another company's, and nobody can change anything through the MCP.

What the user needs#

Before granting access, check the user has all three:

  1. An active Raphael user, from Raphael's office or from a Provider. Clinic users cannot have AI access.
  2. A subscription to an AI assistant that supports custom MCP connectors. Free accounts usually do not. Recommended, in this order:
    • Claude (by Anthropic), in its app or at claude.ai, or Claude Code.
    • ChatGPT (by OpenAI).
    • Grok (by xAI).
    • Gemini (by Google), for now only with Gemini CLI on the computer; Gemini on the web cannot connect yet.

The details of each are in Connect your AI assistant.

  1. An authenticator app on their phone: Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or another. Raphael always asks for it, on top of the password.

And one thing you give them: the address of Raphael's MCP server. It always ends in /mcp.

The levels#

Level in the listWhat the assistant sees
NoneNothing. Every user's default.
Summary (no patient data)All of their company's operational data, without the patients' name, address, phone, date of birth or e-mail. A patient is identified only by their internal number.
Detail (with patient data)The same, with those patient details. Requires a BAA (see below).
Full (set by Raphael only)Listed but cannot be chosen. Only Raphael's technical team sets it, and today it is switched off in every environment.

At every level:

  • Read-only. The assistant cannot create, change or delete anything.
  • Same scope as in the application. Raphael's office sees every company; a Provider's user only their own. The patient list and the general catalogs are shared by everybody.
  • Passwords, signatures and photos are never exposed, at any level.

The steps#

  1. Open Admin and the Employees tab.
  2. Find the user and edit them. The AI access (MCP) field only appears when editing an existing user, and only Raphael's administrator sees it.
  3. In AI access (MCP), choose the level.
  4. If you choose Detail (with patient data), BAA on record appears. Type which document proves the BAA with that AI vendor and its date (for example, "BAA OpenAI Enterprise, signed 2026-11-01"). Without it you cannot save.
  5. Press Save.
  6. Tell the user. Give them:
    • the address of Raphael's MCP server;
    • the link to Connect your AI assistant;
    • the notice that they have 7 days to connect their assistant the first time (see below).

To take access away, set it back to None and press Save. The assistant stops answering within a minute and cannot renew its session.

Detail is only granted with written evidence of the BAA

What the assistant queries does not stay in Raphael: it travels to the servers of the company behind that AI and stays stored in the conversation. With Summary, what travels does not say who each patient is. With Detail, their name, address and phone travel. The medical privacy law (HIPAA) requires that company to have signed a BAA with whoever sends it that data. Personal AI accounts do not have one; only some business plans offer it. Grant Detail without that agreement and the patients' data goes to a third party that is not bound to protect it, and that cannot be undone.

The 7 days and the authenticator app#

  • The first time the user connects their assistant, they enrol their authenticator app with a QR code. They have 7 days from the moment you grant access.
  • After that without enrolling, the page stops them and you have to grant access again.
  • Every time you change a user's level, their authenticator app stops counting and they enrol a new one, again with 7 days. On purpose: a level change always requires proof that whoever signs in really is that person.
  • If the user loses their phone and has no recovery codes: set their level to None, press Save, set back the level they had and press Save. The next time they connect they will enrol a new app.

Which assistants Raphael recognises#

From the user's own computer (Claude Code, Gemini CLI and other desktop tools), Raphael accepts any assistant: the connection comes back to the user's own machine and nobody else can pick it up.

From the web, Raphael only accepts, without asking, these four domains, verified against each vendor:

DomainAssistant
chatgpt.comChatGPT
claude.aiClaude
claude.comClaude (the new address Anthropic announced)
grok.comGrok

If the user tries to connect a web assistant from another domain, Raphael does not let it in: their assistant tells them the domain "is not known to this server yet" and to ask Raphael's administrator to approve it. Raphael records that domain and the assistant's name in a pending list.

How another domain is approved: ask Raphael's technical team, with the assistant's name and the domain. The team checks the domain really belongs to that AI vendor and, if so, approves it. The user then connects their assistant again and gets in. It is not approved from the application.

Why not just any website

At the end of the sign-in, Raphael hands an access code to the address the assistant gave. If any address were accepted, somebody could register a fake "assistant" with their own server and send the director a link to Raphael's real page: one click on Allow would give that third party their access, and at Detail the patients' data. The list of verified domains closes that door.

What can go wrong#

  • I do not see the AI access (MCP) field. Only Raphael's administrator sees it, and only when editing a user, not when creating one. Create the user first, then edit them.
  • It will not save with Detail. BAA on record is missing.
  • The field is disabled. That user has the Full level, which is not changed here.
  • A clinic user cannot be given access. AI access is for Raphael's office and for a Provider's users.
  • The user says the time to enrol their app is over. Set their level to None, press Save and grant the level again. Ask them to connect in the following days.
  • The user says their assistant "is not recognised" or its domain "is not known". They use a web assistant outside the list of four. Ask Raphael's technical team to review and approve that domain, or suggest one from the list.
  • The user says they cannot sign in. Check they are active, have no pending password change, their company is not deactivated and their level is not None.
