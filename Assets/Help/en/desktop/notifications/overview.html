What the system tells you, and why

The three ways something that happened on the road reaches your screen, and the rule that governs all of them.

whyNew in 1.3.0

A trip happens on the road. The office sees it on a screen. Everything between those two things is the notices.

Without them, a dispatcher only learns that a driver reached a clinic door by asking, or by looking. With them, the system says what happened at the moment it happened — and says which of those things needs somebody to act.

The three surfaces#

The same fact shows up in three different ways, and each one answers a different question.

AnswersWhere it is
The bellIs there anything I have not seen? Anything unattended?Top right, always visible
Live alertsWhat just happened, right now?Cards in the bottom right corner
The Notification CentreWhat happened this shift? What happened with this trip?The full inbox, in a tab or its own window

All three look at the same set of notices. Nothing appears in one and is missing from the others: what you miss when an alert closes itself is still in the bell and still in the Centre.

An office notice is one notice for everybody#

The same cancellation is not stored twelve times for twelve dispatchers. It is stored once and the whole office reads it.

That has two consequences worth knowing from the start:

  • Read is yours, and this computer's. Marking a notice as read does not mark it for your colleagues. See Read and unread.
  • Confirming belongs to the team. When somebody confirms a notice it is confirmed for everybody, because it is the signal that the office has taken charge. See Will Call.

The rule that never bends#

Nothing is announced before it is true

The notice goes out after the fact is stored. If you see "Trip cancelled", the trip is cancelled. It is not an intention or a proposal.

And the other way round: if something fails in the notification system, the operation does not change. A cancelled trip stays cancelled even if the notice never reached anybody. So when in doubt, the truth is in Dispatch, not in the inbox.

Notices expire#

An office notice is visible for 12 hours from the moment it was created, which is what a shift lasts. Somebody arriving in the morning after the night shift still sees what happened; the day before yesterday changes none of their decisions.

Anything that needs to outlive that is marked explicitly. See Keeping and cleanup.

What the system does not do#

So that nobody waits for it and is surprised:

  • No SMS and no email. Everything happens inside the applications.
  • No delay detection, no route deviation, no GPS loss. It reports facts somebody recorded, not things it infers.
  • No split by Provider. Whoever is in the office broadcast sees every notice, not only their Provider's.
  • It does not record who confirmed. The office sees Confirmed, but not whose hand it was.
