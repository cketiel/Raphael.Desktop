Sound and silence

Choose what makes a sound, and silence the corner for 15 minutes without losing anything that arrives meanwhile.

how-to

Live alerts can make a sound, and you decide which ones. You can also quiet them for a while without losing anything.

Choosing what makes a sound#

Open "Alert preferences" from the Notification Centre — or from here: Open alert preferences. There are three switches, one per class of notice:

  1. Sound when somebody has to take charge (Will Call) — the notice with a patient waiting behind it.
  2. Sound on cancellations — something went wrong and somebody should know.
  3. Sound as trips move along — the day breathing: started, arrived, on board, completed.

Each one turns on and off separately. In a noisy office the usual setting is the first one on and the third one off.

Silencing for 15 minutes#

The "Silence alerts for 15 minutes" button switches the corner off for that long. Below it you can see what time the silence ends, and "Resume alerts" lifts it early if you need to.

Silencing is not the same as not receiving

Silencing only keeps the cards off the screen. Everything that arrives during those 15 minutes still reaches the bell and the Notification Centre, with its red number and its yellow mark.

Nothing is lost, and there is no case where silencing leaves a patient unattended: what is switched off is the interruption, not the notice. That is why the button lasts 15 minutes and not "until I turn it back on".

What can go wrong#

You silenced and forgot. Alerts come back on their own when the time is up. Meanwhile the bell keeps counting: look there.

No sound even though the switch is on. The sound comes from Windows. Check the system volume, and that Raphael is not muted in the volume mixer.

You want a colleague to have your settings. These preferences belong to this computer and this user: they have to set them on theirs.
