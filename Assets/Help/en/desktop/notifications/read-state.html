Read and unread

Marking notices as read lowers your red number. It is yours and this computer's: it does not affect colleagues and does not follow you to another machine.

how-toNew in 1.3.0

Marking a notice as read lowers the red number on the bell. It is how you get to zero at the end of a shift and start the next one knowing what is new.

The steps#

  • Opening a notice marks it read.
  • "Mark as read" and "Mark as unread" change one without opening it.
  • "Mark all as read" clears the counter in one go.
  • "Mark all as unread" undoes that, if you pressed it by accident.

Leaving something unread is the usual way of telling yourself "come back to this later".

Read is yours; attended belongs to everybody

An office notice is stored once for the whole office. If "read" lived on the server, you opening it would mark it seen for your twelve colleagues at the same time, and nobody would look at it again.

So read stays on your computer: it is how you organise yourself, not a fact about the team. The team fact is confirming, and that one does go to the server and counts for everybody. See Attending a Will Call.

What can go wrong#

You sit at another computer and the counter is high again. That is expected: read does not travel with you. The notices are the same; what does not travel is your mark.

You marked everything read and wanted to review something. The notices are still there: switch to the right tab and find it. Read hides nothing, it only lowers a number.

The red number is zero but a yellow mark remains. They are different things: you have seen everything, but there is something nobody has attended. Go to the "Needs action" tab.
