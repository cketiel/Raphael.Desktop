Keeping a notice, and the automatic cleanup

Why notices disappear on their own, what "keep" actually does, and why it is not the same as archiving an email.

whyNew in 1.3.0

Notices do not pile up forever. Each one is born with an expiry date, and a cleanup deletes it after a while. Except the ones somebody decides to keep.

How long each thing lasts#

For whomShown forDeleted after
The office12 hours7 days past expiry
The driver12 hours7 days past expiry
The patient7 days30 days
The external system7 days30 days

Twelve hours is a shift. Somebody who was offline for five minutes, or who comes in the morning after the night shift, still sees what happened. The day before yesterday changes none of their decisions, and an inbox with three weeks in it stops being read.

The clock runs from when the notice was created, not from when you opened it. A notice from 8:00 leaves the inbox at 20:00 whether you looked at it or not.

Keeping does not mean what it means in email#

In a mail client, archiving means take it off my list. Here it means the opposite:

Keep = the cleanup will never delete this record.

The buttons say exactly that: "Keep: the cleanup will never delete it" and "Stop keeping: it ages like any other".

Keeping does not take it out of the inbox

Keeping does not change the expiry. The notice still leaves the office inbox after 12 hours, like every other. What changes is that the row survives in the system instead of being deleted seven days later.

It is for what will have to be answered later: a trip a patient disputes, a cancellation under investigation. If you keep a notice expecting to still see it in the list tomorrow, that will not happen; what you have done is preserve the evidence.

It is a decision about the record, not about your copy#

Keeping counts for the whole system. It is not like marking read, which is yours and your computer's: it is a decision about that record, and it affects everybody.

Any internal user can keep and stop keeping. Removing a kept record entirely is not: that belongs to administration.

Kept records grow without a ceiling#

It is the only list in the system that is never cleaned. So somebody has to look at it now and then and decide what still deserves to be there. See Administration.

Deleting a kept record cannot be undone

It deletes the row, its recipients and its data, for good. And somebody once decided it was worth keeping. Every deletion is written down with who did it, when, and how many rows.
