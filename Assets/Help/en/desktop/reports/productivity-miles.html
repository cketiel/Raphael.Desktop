The miles — odometer, with a patient, occupied and empty

How each mile of the report is measured, how odometer, GPS and planned differ, and which ones are paid.

referenceNew in 1.23.0

A shift's miles are measured three ways. The odometer rules; the GPS and the route share the miles out and find errors.

MeasureHow it is obtainedWhat for
OdometerPull-in reading − Pull-out reading, typed by the driverThe shift's total
GPSSum of the distances between the phone's positions. Jumps above 100 mph do not countMeasuring each leg, and checking
PlannedSum of the legs of the performed routeFilling the GPS gaps, and checking

With a patient, occupied and empty#

The day is cut into legs between one Perform and the next. A pickup's Perform adds a patient and a dropoff's takes one away, so each leg's riders are known. A leg's miles come from the GPS; with fewer than two positions, from its planned distance.

FigureFormula
Miles with a patientSum of (leg miles × patients on board). A mile with 1 patient is 1; with 2 patients it is 2; with none, 0. They are the ones paid
Occupied milesSum of the legs with 1 or more patients, each mile once. Never above the odometer
Empty milesOdometer − occupied miles
OccupancyOccupied miles ÷ odometer

Odometer = occupied + empty. The occupied miles are taken off, not the miles with a patient, because the vehicle drives once the mile that carries two. Only completed trips count. Companions do not count.

An example#

LegMilesPatientsOccupiedWith a patient
Garage → picks up Ana12000
Ana on board, picks up Luis8188
Ana and Luis on board1021020
Luis on board5155
Back to the garage15000
Total502333

Odometer 50, occupied 23 (46% occupancy), empty 27 and 33 miles with a patient: more than the occupied ones, because the 10 miles with Ana and Luis count 20. A driver at $1.00 a mile earns $33.00.

Each shift's calculation#

Calculation, beside the miles, opens this same sum with the day's numbers: the bars of the odometer, with a patient, GPS and planned with their totals; the subtractions; and the legs one by one with their patients. An asterisk marks a leg without GPS. If that day's positions were deleted, it says so.

Off-route miles#

Pull-out − the vehicle's last reading. It is what moved between two shifts, with no route. It is not part of the shift's miles and is not paid.

Which miles are paid#

Only the miles with a patient, and only to a driver paid per mile. Correcting an odometer changes the vehicle's miles but not the pay: miles with a patient come from the GPS and the patients on board.

The values that set the warnings off can be edited

The platform administrator changes them in What do the warnings mean?: each warning has its limit there and can be turned off. How to do it: Changing the limits.

What can go wrong#

What you seeWhat happens and what to do
Huge miles, or 0% occupancyThe odometer is mistyped. Correct it
A dash instead of the milesA reading is missing, or the Pull-in is below the Pull-out
GPS far belowThe phone had no signal or no location permission
More empty miles than usualSee whether there are trips without a Perform: only completed ones count
