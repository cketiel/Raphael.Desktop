How long the driver waits

Why an arrival is never shown too early, how long that leaves the driver sitting, and where to see it.

whyNew in 1.8.0

A pickup is never shown with the vehicle arriving more than 15 minutes before the hour the patient was promised. On a return trip (Return) the margin is 5 minutes.

When the calculation gives an earlier arrival than that, the ETA is raised to the limit. What is not shortened is the drive: the road takes what it takes. The difference between the two hours is the driver sitting in the vehicle outside a house with the engine off.

Why we tell you#

That number used to be worked out, used to raise the ETA, and thrown away. You looked at a column of perfectly reasonable hours with no way of knowing that one of your drivers was going to be parked for two of them.

On a morning with more trips than vehicles, that is capacity burnt in silence.

Where to see it#

On the arrival hour. An amber tag sits next to the ETA with the waiting time: 08:45 ⏱ 45 m. Hover it and it spells out the three hours: when the driver could be there, when the rule sets the arrival, and what was promised.

**In the Wait column.** The same figure as a column, to sort and compare at a glance. It starts switched off: turn it on with the cog in the first column and it appears just after the ETA.

In the row's background. From 30 minutes of waiting, the whole row turns amber.

That threshold is not set from the app

It is a system setting (Scheduling.EarlyArrivalWaitHighlightMinutes) and has no screen today. If 30 minutes is too long or too short for your operation, ask whoever administers the server.

Amber and red never collide

Red says the vehicle arrives late for what was promised. Amber says it arrives too early and waits. A row cannot be both, so the colour always means one thing.

The route as time bars#

The bars button, next to the cog in the first column, swaps the grid for a list of bars: the name, the bar across all the width there is, and the stop's number at the far end. The other columns fold away for as long as that view lasts.

Every hour on a bar has its own mark:

MarkWhat it is
Tick at the far leftthe hour the vehicle leaves the previous stop
Green stretchthe drive
Red tickthe hour the driver could be at the door
Red stretchthe wait; where the stretch is wide enough it carries the minutes inside
Heavy green tickthe ETA: the arrival the route shows
Pale green stretchthe margin the rule keeps in front
Tick at the far rightthe hour the patient was promised

If the vehicle arrives late, that last stretch is drawn dark red and the two ticks swap places: the promised hour first, then the ETA at the end, overshooting it.

Hover the bar

The full breakdown opens, a colour chip to each line: when it leaves the previous stop, how long the drive takes, when the driver could be at the door, how long they wait, the ETA, the margin — or the delay — and the promised hour. The bar is for seeing it at a glance; the breakdown is for deciding.

In one pass you can see which stops have a red stretch and how much of the bar it takes.

The same button is on every row. Pressing it on one stop puts the screen into that same bar view and draws that stop's bar; the other rows show their three hours as text, so none is left blank.

  • Press the one in the header and every stop draws its bar at once.
  • The moment one stop is showing a bar, the header icon already reads "back to the grid", with an orange dot and a tooltip saying how many are like that. Pressing it returns everything to the grid of figures.
  • Pull-out and Pull-in have no bar. They are the garage departure and return: nobody was promised an hour there, so there is no rule to draw. They show their hour as it is.
