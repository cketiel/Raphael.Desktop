Schedules — planning the day

What this screen is for, the three zones, both grids with all their columns and colours, and why it moves on its own.

reference

Schedules is where a trip stops merely existing and gets a vehicle behind it.

You come in with a list of patients who do not know whether anybody is collecting them, and you leave with each one assigned to a driver and given an hour. Everything else on this screen — the colours, the bars, the map — is there so you can make that decision knowing what you need to know.

Open Schedules

SectionWhat it is
ScheduleThe day's planning: the open trips and the routes they get assigned to
RevenueThe day in money
GraphsThe day in charts

What follows describes the Schedule section.

The three zones#

The screen is split in three, with two splitters you can drag:

  • Top left — the route. The stops of one route, in the order the driver will drive them.
  • Top right — the map. That route's stops and the vehicle running it.
  • Bottom — the unrouted trips. Every trip of the day with no vehicle yet. On a normal day that is between 300 and 400.

The splitters are there so you can decide where to work. Planning? Drag the horizontal one up and give the trip list room. Following a driver? Drag the vertical one left and the map takes the screen.

The top grid shows one route

Whichever you picked in the selector. Changing route changes that whole grid and the map; the list below is the entire day and does not depend on the route you picked.

The filters at the top#

ControlWhat it does
DateThe day you are planning. Changes both grids
GroupNarrows the route selector to one vehicle group. The circle is the group's colour
RouteThe route whose stops you see above. Each option's background is its group's colour
Show performed eventsStops already done are hidden by default, so you see what is left. Tick it to see them all

To the right of the selector is the summary: how many trips the route carries and the estimated miles.

The route grid#

Each row is one stop, not one trip. A trip puts down two: the pickup and the dropoff.

Pull-out and Pull-in are the garage departure and return. No patient is waiting on either, they are always first and last, and they cannot be dragged or taken off the route.

The columns that decide things#

ColumnWhat it is
PickupThe hour the patient was promised for collection
ApptThe hour of their appointment, on the dropoff
ETAWhen the vehicle will get there, by the calculation. It is the hour lateness is measured against, and the one the patient sees on their tracking page
WaitHow long the driver will stand at that door waiting for the hour. Off by default; switch it on with the cog
Distance / TravelMiles and time of the drive from the previous stop to this one
The numbered circleThe stop's place in the round, and the same number that marks it on the map

Columns are chosen with the cog in the first header. Your choice is kept on your machine, not in the system: every dispatcher has their own.

The colours#

What you seeWhat it means
Red rowThe ETA is more than fifteen minutes past the promised hour: the driver arrives late
Amber rowThe driver arrives too early and will sit there for more than half an hour
⏱ 45 m beside the ETAExactly how long they will wait at that stop
Red textThe trip is cancelled
Green flagThe driver arrived at that stop
Purple flagThe driver performed that stop

Red is a promise being broken

A red row means that at that hour there is a patient waiting and the vehicle will not be there. It is measured against the pickup hour on a pickup and the appointment hour on a dropoff, with a fifteen-minute window.

Red and amber never coincide

Either the vehicle arrives late, or it arrives early and waits. A stop cannot be both, so the colour always means one thing. Everything about the amber is in How long the driver waits.

The two views of this grid#

The bars button, next to the cog, swaps the grid of columns for a timeline: every stop drawn to scale, the drive in green, the wait in amber, and each hour marked in its place. In one pass you see where a route's dead time piles up.

The same button is on every row, to open a single stop. Explained in full in How long the driver waits.

What you can do on each row#

ButtonWhat it does
Take off the routeRemoves the whole trip — both its stops — and sends it back down. See Take a trip off a route
Undo performedOn stops already done: reverts the driver's arrival or performance
Show as a barOpens that stop as a timeline

And dragging a row changes the route's order: see Change the order of the stops.

The unrouted trips grid#

This is the whole day, not one route. In the first column's header is the counter: how many trips are still unrouted and, in brackets, how many of those are Will Calls. It stays there as you scroll.

Each row has its buttons on the left, and they act on their own row: no need to select it first.

ButtonWhat it does
RoutePuts the trip on the route selected above. See Route a trip
CancelThe trip is not happening. Not the same as taking it off a route
Make Will CallThe trip waits for the patient to say they are ready
Activate Will CallThe patient rang: an hour is set and it rejoins the normal queue
EditOpens the trip
HistoryWhat was changed on it, when, and by whom

Of the two Will Call buttons only one is ever shown, the one that matches the trip's state. Neither appears on a cancelled trip: there is nothing to promise anybody.

The colours#

What you seeWhat it means
Purple bar on the leftThis is the selected row
Red textCancelled by the driver, with a no-show reason
Grey italic textCancelled by the office
The row is marked and leaves on its ownThat trip has just been cancelled while you had it in front of you

Select before you route

Clicking a trip draws its pickup and dropoff on the map beside the stops of the route you have chosen. That is how you see whether it is on the way before deciding, not after.

The map#

It marks the stops of the chosen route and, if you have selected a trip below, that trip's pickup and dropoff too.

The vehicle appears as a heading arrow inside a coloured circle, pointing where it is travelling. If it stops, the arrow keeps pointing where it was going — a van parked facing south still faces south. What tells you it has stopped is the colour, which is the speed.

The driver reports position every 30 seconds, so the arrow moves slowly and continuously between one report and the next: that is an animation, not the exact live position. For the last measured position, its speed and its hour, hover it.

The colour is the speed, not the state of the trip

A blue circle is a stationary vehicle — at a light, at a patient's door, or parked. It says nothing about whether the trip is going well.

This screen moves on its own#

There is no refresh button because there is no need for one. Several dispatchers work the same day, drivers report from the road, and all three zones update independently without anybody reloading anything.
