Schedules — planning the day

The three areas of the screen, what is in each grid, and what its colours mean.

reference

Schedules is where a trip stops being a request and gets a vehicle behind it.

Open Schedules

SectionWhat it is
ScheduleThe day's planning: the open trips and the routes they are assigned to
RevenueThe day in money
GraphsThe day in charts

What follows describes the Schedule section.

The three areas, and why you can move them#

The screen is divided in three, with two dividers you can drag:

  • Top left — the route. The stops of one route, in the order the driver will do them.
  • Top right — the map. That route's stops and the vehicle running it.
  • Bottom — the unrouted trips. Every trip of the day with no vehicle yet. On a normal day that is three to four hundred.

The dividers are there so you decide where to work. Planning? Drag the horizontal divider up and gain room for the trip list. Following a driver? Drag the vertical one left and the map takes the screen.

The top grid shows one route

Whichever is selected in the route picker. Changing route changes that whole grid and the map; the trip list at the bottom is the whole day and does not depend on the route you picked.

The filters at the top#

ControlWhat it does
DateThe day you are planning. Changes both grids
GroupFilters the route picker to one vehicle group. The circle is the group's colour
RouteThe route whose stops you see above. Each option's background is its group's colour
Display performed eventsPerformed stops are hidden by default, so you see what is left to do. Tick it to see them all

To the right of the route picker is the route summary: how many trips it carries and the estimated mileage.

The route grid#

Each row is one stop, not one trip. A trip puts two on the route: the pickup and the dropoff.

Pull-out and Pull-in are the vehicle leaving the garage and coming back. No patient is waiting on either, they are always first and last, and they cannot be dragged.

The colours in this grid#

What you seeWhat it means
Row on a light red backgroundThe ETA is more than 15 minutes past the promised hour: the driver will be late to that stop
Text in redThe trip is cancelled
Green flagThe driver arrived at that stop
Purple flagThe driver performed that stop

Red is a promise being broken

A red row is not decoration: it means that at that hour there is a patient waiting and the vehicle will not be there. It is measured against the pickup hour on a pickup and the appointment hour on a dropoff, with a 15-minute window.

The columns of this grid are chosen with the cog button in the first header. Your choice is saved on your machine, not in the system: every dispatcher has their own.

The unrouted trips grid#

The header of the first column carries the count: how many trips are still unrouted and, in brackets, how many of those are Will Calls. It stays there as you scroll down.

Each row has its action buttons on the left. They act on their own row: you do not have to select it first.

What you seeWhat it means
Purple bar down the leftThis is the selected row
Text in redCancelled by the driver, with a no-show reason
Grey italic textCancelled by the office
The row is marked and leaves on its ownThat trip has just been cancelled while you had it in front of you

The map#

It marks the stops of the selected route and, if you have selected a trip from the list below, its pickup and dropoff too — so you can see whether it fits that route before deciding.

The vehicle appears as a heading arrow inside a coloured circle, pointing the way it is driving. If it stops, the arrow stays pointing where it was going — a van parked facing south is still facing south. That it is stopped is told by the colour, which is its speed.

The driver reports position every 30 seconds, so the arrow moves slowly and continuously between one report and the next: that movement is an animation, not the exact live position. For the last measured position, its speed and the time, hover over it.

The colour is speed, not the state of the trip

A blue circle is a vehicle standing still — at a light, at a patient's door, or parked. It says nothing about whether the trip is going well.
