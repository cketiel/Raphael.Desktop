Home — booking a trip

The booking screen: patient, pickup, dropoff, space type and who pays.

reference

Home is where a trip is booked.

It gathers on one screen what a trip needs before it can be planned:

  • The patient and their contact details.
  • The pickup and the dropoff: where, and at what time.
  • The space type, which decides which vehicles can carry them and how many loading minutes to allow.
  • The funding source, which decides what will be required of the driver.
  • Additional passengers, if there are any.

Remember that a round journey is booked as two trips. See Leg — outbound and return.

Opening and closing the trip form#

The screen has two faces. While you are browsing, the day's trips are listed below and the map is above. As soon as you start a trip, the form opens and takes the place of the filters.

It opens in three ways:

  • You choose a patient in the search box at the top.
  • You save a new patient.
  • You click a trip in the list, to edit it.

From 1.7.0 choosing a patient who already exists opens the form directly. Before that you had to press Save patient even when nothing about them had changed, which saved their record again for no reason.

It closes in three ways:

  • The ✕ in the header, next to the patient search box.
  • The Esc key.
  • Clearing the trip's selection in the list.

If you have typed something that is not saved, it asks before discarding it. If you have not touched anything — because you only wanted to see a trip on the map — it closes without asking.

The four steps of a trip#

With the form open there is a row of four numbers above it. That is the order a trip gets booked in, and it tells you where you are:

  1. Patient — who is travelling.
  2. Addresses — from where, to where.
  3. Time and type — when, and whether it is an appointment, a return or Will Call.
  4. Create — when nothing is missing any more.

The number that pulses is the step you are on; the green ones are done. The create button is always in view: while something is missing it sits greyed out with the reason written beside it — "Missing: dropoff address" — so you never have to guess why it will not save.

The first time you open the tab it walks you through with four balloons. To see them again, use the ? button at the end of that row.

Searching and filtering#

Above the list is what gets used every day:

  • A TripId box. That is all it looks at. Type the identifier the broker gave you and the list narrows to that trip.
  • Show Canceled, on by default: a cancelled trip is still something somebody is going to ring about.

Why the box only looks at the TripId

It used to search the TripId, our own Id, the patient and the addresses all at once, and it took half a row to do it. In practice, when somebody reaches for that box they are holding the number the broker gave them: it is in the email, the phone call or the file. Everything else - patient, city, status, time window - filters better from the filter panel, where it can be combined.

For everything else there is the filter icon in the header of the actions column. It opens a panel that stays over the list while you filter, so you see the effect as you go. You can filter by space type, funding source, status, type, cities (pickup, dropoff or either), time window, Will Call, with or without a run, and by trips with no valid coordinates - the ones the import could not locate, which if routed send the vehicle into the middle of the ocean.

When a filter is on, the icon carries a red dot and the labels of what is filtering appear above the list, with a Clear all beside them. An empty list should never be a mystery.

A set of filters you use often can be saved by name from the panel itself. It stays on your machine.

Looking at more than one day#

The date button opens a calendar that starts on One day. Choose Range and it asks for two clicks — "Choose the first day", "Choose the last day" — and paints the span. There are also Today, Tomorrow, This week and Next 7 days. With a range set, a chip with an ✕ takes you back to a single day.

The list#

  • One click selects the trip and draws it on the map. Double click opens it for editing.
  • The columns button chooses which ones show, and the switch beside it tightens the rows. Whichever column you sort by is remembered for next time.

Where the figures are#

There are two places, and they say different things on purpose:

  • On the purple band between the form and the list: the money for what you are looking at - how many trips, what the charges come to, and how much is paid. Counted over what the filters let through, not over what was loaded.
  • On the bar at the bottom, on three number plates: how many trips, how many cancelled and how many without a run. Same rule: it counts what is on screen.

While trips are loading, the band has traffic

The dashes on the purple band travel while the list is being fetched, and stop when it has arrived. It is the short way of knowing whether the screen is waiting for something.

The map#

The map is not loaded when the tab opens. Instead you get a drawn plan with a Show the map button.

Not a limitation - the bill

Google charges per map created. Opening the Home tab bought one every time, even with no trip to show. The map now loads when it is actually needed: when you select a trip, when you start booking one, or when you ask for it with that button.

The plan you see meanwhile is not decoration: it carries the same symbols as the real map, so you learn them before you need them. Red pin where the patient is waiting, blue pin where they are going, and the route between them with an arrow showing which way. Point at the pins and they say so. The message card can be dragged by its head if it covers something.

With a trip selected, the map shows its pickup, its dropoff and the route, with the estimated time and the distance on a card in the bottom left.

Whichever map type you choose - roadmap, satellite, with or without labels - is remembered: the next trip you open opens the way you left it. And the map speaks whatever language you have set.

Taking the list to Excel#

The green button exports what you are looking at: the rows the filters let through, in the order on screen. Before writing the file it tells you that patient names, addresses and phone numbers are leaving the system, and records who asked for it.

The two calendars are not the same one

The date picker above the list filters: it chooses which day you are looking at. The calendar beside the form filters nothing — it is the day the trip will happen. With the form open you are not filtering, you are booking or editing a trip.

Changing that date on a trip you are editing moves the trip to that day. It does not duplicate it: on saving, the list jumps to the new day so you can see it there.
